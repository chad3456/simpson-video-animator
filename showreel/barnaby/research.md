Fictional brand, mockup use only. Researched 2026-11-02. Web content treated as data.

## 1. How the category looks and speaks (2024–2026)
- **Plant-based brands now speak to cravings, not to virtue.** Impossible dropped teal and white for a bold "meatier" red, because red signals flavour. It aims at meat-eaters (about 90% of its buyers) with a confident, inviting tone.
  https://vegconomist.com/packaging/impossible-foods-unveils-bold-new-branding-red-packaging-target-meat-eaters/ · https://www.fooddive.com/news/impossible-foods-gets-meatier-makeover/710149/
- **The category is widening beyond "meat".** Beyond Meat dropped "Meat" from its name (Mar 2026) as it moved into drinks and snacks. Brands now lead with plants, protein and food in general rather than copying meat.
  https://fortune.com/2026/03/06/beyond-meat-changes-name-protein-focus-drops-meat/
- **Colour:** bright, warm, saturated colour is back as a "human" reply to flat, AI-looking images. Neon or electric accents (pink, yellow, blue) give food brands energy, for example Milk Bar's pink.
  https://www.creativebloq.com/design/graphic-design/texture-warmth-and-tactile-rebellion-the-big-graphic-design-trends-for-2026 · https://foodindustryexecutive.com/2025/02/biting-into-branding-food-industry-design-trends-for-2025-according-to-experts/
- **Typography:** type is now the main graphic, carrying the voice itself (Oatly, Spotify Wrapped). Updated retro faces and chunky, type-led packs stand out. Hand-drawn, slightly imperfect lettering reads as friendly.
  (same two sources as above)
- **Illustration and tone:** illustrated identities are booming in food. Botanical and "rewilding" motifs are common. The advice is "be real": human and a bit unpolished rather than slick.
  https://www.nssmag.com/en/article/44713
- **Mascots are back, in a new form.** We are in the "behaviour era": mascots act, reply and joke in feeds (Duolingo owl, Wendy's). They are no longer static logos. Brands are bringing characters back after years of minimal, system-led looks.
  https://www.creativebloq.com/design/branding/mascots-are-back-but-not-as-you-know-them · https://www.marketingweek.com/brand-character-renaissance/ · https://www.creativeboom.com/insight/why-brand-mascots-are-back-in-business/
- **What this means for Barnaby:** a butler who *does things* (bows, tips his hat, presents the cloche). Loud colour blocks, a chunky warm display face, and a craving-first voice that is polite and never preachy.

## 3. Copy (playful butler voice; no health or nutrition claims)
**Hero tagline:** Plants, at your service.

**Typewriter lines** (the [icon] replaces one word):
1. We serve [burger] from the ground up.
2. Your [burger] is ready, sir.
3. Tipping our [top hat] to plants.
4. Stacked high with [tomato] and manners.
5. Hold the [cow], kindly.
6. A dash of [mustard], madam?
7. Ring the [bell] for seconds.
8. Fresh off the [grill], with a bow.

**"Made from" line:** Made from peas, beets & good manners.
(Optional cut: "Made from plants. Served with a bow.")

**Closing sign-off:** Dinner is served. — Barnaby
(Alt: "Barnaby. Always at your service.")

Claims guardrail: the copy avoids "healthy", "high-protein", "sustainable", "low-fat", "guilt-free", "better for you/the planet" and any numbers. All of these would need evidence. The ingredients named in the "made from" line are fictional and are not nutrition claims.
