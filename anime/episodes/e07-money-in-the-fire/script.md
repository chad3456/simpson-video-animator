# Dostoevsky at the Coin Laundry E7: "Money in the Fire"

*Late November. A classmate has seen Mio at the laundry with "an old guy," and the gossip has followed her all week. Over Nastasya Filippovna, who throws a hundred thousand roubles into the fire, and proud Aglaya, who mocks the prince because she loves him, Mio says one sentence too many. Itsuki answers with the most careful thing he can think of, and they have their first quarrel.*

Book: The Idiot (1869). Format: anime, about 15 minutes. Original story. Discusses The Idiot (1869); Dostoevsky passages are our own renderings of the public-domain texts.

Original characters, designs and music. Not affiliated with any studio.

**MIO**: The last chapter. Myshkin and Rogozhin, in that dark house, at night. Lying side by side on cushions on the floor, next to the curtain. And behind the curtain is...

**ITSUKI**: Yes.

**MIO**: And Rogozhin starts shaking and shouting, and Myshkin just strokes his hair. All night. Stroking the hair of the man who did it. And when they find them in the morning, the prince doesn't recognise anyone. He's gone back to being an idiot, properly this time. They send him back to Switzerland.

**MIO**: Why did Gramps love a book that ends like that? He read it every winter.

**ITSUKI**: ...Maybe because for six hundred pages there was a man like that in the world. Even if it was only in a book. Even if the world couldn't keep him.

**ITSUKI**: Dostoevsky tried to write a perfectly good man, and he couldn't make him win. But he didn't make him bad, either, to make the ending easier. He let goodness lose and still be good. I think that's very brave.

**MIO**: Gramps wrote on the last page: "Read again next winter." Every year. There are eleven of them. Eleven winters, in different pencils.

**ITSUKI**: Then this is the twelfth winter.

**MIO**: ...I'll write it. "Read again next winter." In my pencil.

**MIO**: But first. The fire. I have to talk about the fire.

**ITSUKI**: Nastasya Filippovna's party.

**MIO**: It's her birthday. And every man in the room is trying to buy her. One wants to marry her off with seventy-five thousand roubles so he can marry someone respectable. Ganya will marry her for the money, even though he's ashamed of her. And then Rogozhin bursts in, drunk, with a hundred thousand roubles wrapped in a newspaper.

**MIO**: Like she's at an auction. And she knows it. That's the worst part. She's the only one in the room who says what's happening.

**ITSUKI**: And Myshkin asks her to marry him.

**MIO**: Myshkin asks her to marry him. Not to buy her. To save her. And she says no, because she thinks she'd ruin him. She runs off with Rogozhin instead.

**MIO**: But before she goes, she throws the hundred thousand roubles in the fire.

*INSERT: the book "The Idiot".*

> If you pull it out with your bare hands, Ganya, it is yours. All of it.
>
> *Grandfather's pencil:* Everyone watches the money. / Watch Ganya's face. / — G.

**ITSUKI**: And Ganya doesn't move. He stands there, watching it burn, and then he faints.

**MIO**: He faints! And she pulls it out herself with the fire tongs and throws it at him. It's the most dramatic thing I've ever read. I read it on the bus and the man next to me asked if I was all right.

**ITSUKI**: Were you?

**MIO**: I was cheering. Quietly.

**MIO**: Is she mad? Nastasya?

**ITSUKI**: She was taken in by a rich man when she was a child, and he made her what everyone calls her. And she agrees with them. She thinks she's ruined. So she'd rather burn everything than let someone kind be kind to her.

**MIO**: That's what the Underground Man did. To Liza.

**ITSUKI**: ...It's the same wound. Different book.

**MIO**: And then there's Aglaya. My favourite.

**ITSUKI**: Of course she's your favourite.

**MIO**: The youngest daughter of the general. Proud, beautiful, very rude. She mocks Myshkin all the time. She calls him a poor knight. She reads a poem at him in front of everyone to embarrass him. She sends him a hedgehog!

**ITSUKI**: The hedgehog.

**MIO**: An actual live hedgehog! In a basket! As a message! And nobody knows what it means, and everyone argues about the hedgehog for a whole chapter.

**ITSUKI**: And the poor knight. The poem.

**MIO**: Tell me about the poem. I didn't get the poem. Gramps just wrote "Pushkin, of course" and a smiley face.

**ITSUKI**: It's a Pushkin ballad. A knight has a vision once, of something holy and beautiful, and after that he never lifts his visor to look at any woman again. He rides about doing good deeds, faithful to one vision his whole life, and everyone thinks he's mad.

**ITSUKI**: Aglaya reads it aloud in front of everyone, to tease Myshkin. But she reads it so beautifully that everyone goes quiet. She meant it as a joke, and it came out as the truth.

**MIO**: ...That happens.

**ITSUKI**: In books.

**MIO**: In books.

**MIO**: But it's obvious. It's so obvious. Aglaya mocks the prince because she loves him. She can't say it, so she teases him, and she's horrible to him, because if she was nice he'd...

**MIO**: ...Anyway. The hedgehog.

**ITSUKI**: ...The hedgehog.
