# Dostoevsky at the Coin Laundry E4: "The Idea with an Axe"

*October, and a typhoon. Mio has been carrying an ugly thought about the baby for a week. Over Raskolnikov and the theory that carried him to murder, Itsuki admits the theory he once used on himself, and when the power fails, Mio says her thought out loud in the dark.*

Book: Crime and Punishment (1866). Format: anime, about 15 minutes. Original story. Discusses Crime and Punishment (1866); Dostoevsky passages are our own renderings of the public-domain texts.

### [1:38] COIN LAUNDRY HOSHIZORA, INSIDE, NIGHT, TYPHOON

*Thursday, October. Typhoon No. 19.*

**MIO**: You brought the umbrella. You kept it. I'm so proud.

**ITSUKI**: It turned inside out twice on the bridge.

**MIO**: And you still came. In a typhoon. For laundry.

**ITSUKI**: ...I had a lot of laundry.

**MIO**: I finished it. Crime and Punishment. Five hundred pages in eight days. I read it in the bath. The pages went wavy.

**ITSUKI**: Eight days.

**MIO**: It's a thriller. Nobody told me it's a thriller. Gramps always made it sound like homework.

**ITSUKI**: It was printed in a magazine, in parts. People queued for the next issue. It's the first great crime novel where you know who did it on page eighty.

**MIO**: So. Rodion Romanovich Raskolnikov. Ex-student. Lives in a room so small his landlady's maid calls it a cupboard. Hasn't paid rent in months. Lies on a sofa thinking.

**MIO**: Sound like anyone?

**ITSUKI**: My room has a window.

**MIO**: That's a low bar, Underground-san.

**MIO**: The part that made me feel sick wasn't even the murder. It was the dream before it. The horse.

**ITSUKI**: ...The horse.

**MIO**: He dreams he's seven, walking with his father past a tavern, and a crowd of drunk men are beating an old mare because she can't pull a cart full of people. They beat her until she dies. And little Rodya runs through the crowd and throws his arms around her dead head and kisses her eyes.

**ITSUKI**: And he wakes up and says, no, I can't do it. I won't. And for a moment he's free.

**MIO**: So why does he do it anyway?

**ITSUKI**: Because the boy in the dream and the man on the sofa aren't speaking to each other any more. The boy is still in there, crying over the horse. The man has a theory.

**MIO**: Gramps had the dream page folded down. Not underlined. Just folded. Like he couldn't bear to write on it.

**MIO**: And he has a theory. That's the scary part. Not the axe. The theory.

**ITSUKI**: That people are divided into two kinds.

**MIO**: Ordinary people, who are just material. They obey the law and have children. And extraordinary people, like Napoleon, who have the right to step over anything. Even blood. Because history will say they were right.

**MIO**: And then he looks at a horrible old pawnbroker who cheats students, and he thinks: a hundred good deeds could be done with her money. One death, a hundred lives. It's just arithmetic.

**ITSUKI**: Two times two.

**MIO**: The Underground Man would hate him.

**ITSUKI**: The Underground Man would throw a stone at his crystal palace.

**MIO**: But then it doesn't work. He kills her, and her sister Lizaveta walks in. Lizaveta, who was gentle and simple and mended everyone's clothes. And he kills her too. She doesn't even raise her hands to her face.

**ITSUKI**: That's the moment the arithmetic ends.

**MIO**: And he doesn't even spend the money. He hides the purse under a stone in a courtyard and never opens it. He doesn't know how much was in it.

**MIO**: Gramps wrote something next to the stone.

*INSERT: the book "Crime and Punishment".*

> He had not even looked into the purse; he did not know how much was in it.
> He lifted the stone and slid everything underneath.
>
> *Grandfather's pencil:* An idea is a guest. / Do not give it / the best room. / — G.

**ITSUKI**: ...That's the whole book, really.

**MIO**: He lay in that cupboard for a month, feeding the idea. Giving it the best room. By the time he picked up the axe, the idea was the one living there, and he was the guest.

**ITSUKI** (thinking): She says these things as if they're obvious. As if she isn't seventeen.

**MIO**: Did you ever have a theory? A Raskolnikov one?

**ITSUKI**: ...Yes.

**MIO**: Really?

**ITSUKI**: When I was twenty-three, something I'd done was... noticed. For a while. And I decided that from then on I had to be extraordinary, or nothing. That anything ordinary I made would be a kind of crime.

**ITSUKI**: So I made nothing. Five years. It's a very tidy theory. No axe. You just use it on yourself, a little every day.

**MIO**: That's the saddest crime I've ever heard of.

**ITSUKI**: It's not a crime. Nobody died.

**MIO**: Somebody did. A bit.

### [6:40] COIN LAUNDRY HOSHIZORA, INSIDE, NIGHT, TYPHOON

**MIO**: It's easier in the dark. Talking.

**ITSUKI**: ...Yes.

**MIO**: That's why Raskolnikov confesses to Sonya at night, isn't it. In her room, with the candle burning down.

**ITSUKI**: And she doesn't run away. She says, "What have you done to yourself?" Not to them. To yourself.

**MIO**: Can I confess something? It's not an axe. It's much smaller. But it's ugly.

**ITSUKI**: ...You don't have to.

**MIO**: I want to. Before I put it under a stone.

**MIO**: Last week, Mum said they'd move Gramps's boxes to the garage. For the cot. And for one second, I thought: I wish there wasn't going to be a baby.

**MIO**: She hasn't even been born. She's a heartbeat that sounds like a horse. And I wished her away so I could keep a corner of a room that isn't even mine.

**MIO**: What kind of sister thinks that? I've hated myself all week. I thought if I said it, it would become true.

**ITSUKI**: Can I say something about Raskolnikov?

**MIO**: ...It had better be good.

**ITSUKI**: His crime wasn't that he had the thought. Everyone in that city had thoughts like that. There's a student in the tavern who says the exact same thing out loud, as a joke, at the start of the book.

**ITSUKI**: His crime was that he kept it. He took it home to his cupboard and fed it for a month and never told a living soul. He gave it the best room.

**ITSUKI**: You had the thought for one second. And a week later you brought it out into a coin laundry, in a typhoon, and said it out loud to a stranger.

**ITSUKI**: That's not Raskolnikov. That's the opposite. That's lifting the stone.

**MIO**: ...You're not a stranger.

**ITSUKI**: ...Proofreader from the laundry.

**MIO**: Can I ask what you'd do? If you were me.

**ITSUKI**: ...I'd ask if I could help paint the room. The baby's room. I'd make it partly mine that way.

**ITSUKI**: And I'd put the books somewhere she can find them when she's seventeen.

**MIO** (thinking): Gramps, he said seventeen. He imagined her at seventeen. Reading your books.

**MIO**: So you're my Sonya.

**ITSUKI**: I'm... not Sonya.

**MIO**: Who, then?

**ITSUKI**: Porfiry, maybe. The investigator. He knows everything and he never arrests him. He just keeps telling him to go outside. "What you need is air. Air."

**MIO**: You're telling me to go outside? You?

**ITSUKI**: I'm outside. It's a typhoon. I came.

**MIO**: And your theory? Extraordinary or nothing? Are you going to lift that stone?

**ITSUKI**: ...I don't know where I put it.

**MIO**: Yes you do. Everyone keeps it in the same place. A drawer. The one that sticks.

**ITSUKI**: ...How did you know it sticks?

**MIO**: Because Gramps had one too. His first translation got a terrible review in a newspaper in nineteen seventy-four. He kept it in a drawer that stuck for forty years. When we cleared the house, the review was on top of all his prizes.

**ITSUKI**: He kept the bad review on top.

**MIO**: He said it kept the prizes humble.

### [10:02] COIN LAUNDRY HOSHIZORA, INSIDE, NIGHT, RAIN

**MIO**: Light.

**ITSUKI**: Seven years left of his sentence. And they think of them as seven days.

**MIO**: I didn't cry at the murder. I cried at that.

**ITSUKI**: ...Me too.

**ITSUKI**: Razumikhin.

**ITSUKI**: ...I don't bring soup.

**MIO**: You bring a broken umbrella. It's close enough.
