# Dostoevsky at the Coin Laundry E9: "The Ridiculous Man"

*Mid-December. Mio's mother has gone into hospital three weeks early, and for two Thursdays the bench is empty. Alone with Dostoevsky's strangest short story, about a man who had decided nothing mattered until a little girl in the rain proved him wrong, Itsuki remembers the winter he wanted nothing, and keeps the notebook company until she comes back.*

Book: The Dream of a Ridiculous Man (1877). Format: anime, about 15 minutes. Original story. Discusses The Dream of a Ridiculous Man (1877); Dostoevsky passages are our own renderings of the public-domain texts.

*Thursday, mid-December.*

> *Grandfather's pencil:* Kirillov. Right about / the leaf. Wrong about / everything else. / — G.

**ITSUKI** (thinking): Kirillov, in Demons. The engineer who has decided to die, and who stays up all night playing with a neighbour's baby, and says everything is good. Dostoevsky put the happiest speech in the book in the mouth of the saddest man.

**ITSUKI** (thinking): She lent me her grandfather's leaf. She doesn't lend people things. She lends people nicknames.

**ITSUKI** (thinking): Page one goes in the notebook, between her letter and the sock notices. Folded once. The first page I've given anyone in five years, and nobody's here to read it. That's probably the right way to start.

**ITSUKI** (thinking): The Dream of a Ridiculous Man. Twenty pages. She's right. I'll read it here.

**ITSUKI** (thinking): "I am a ridiculous man. Now they call me mad." It starts like the Underground Man. A man nobody listens to, talking to an audience that isn't there.

**ITSUKI** (thinking): He has decided that nothing matters. Not as a mood. As a fact. Nothing in the world makes any difference. So one November night he goes home intending to end his life.

**ITSUKI** (thinking): On the way, a little girl grabs his sleeve in the rain. Eight years old, soaking, crying "Mama, mama." Her mother is dying. She's looking for anyone at all. And he shouts at her and stamps his foot and walks on.

**ITSUKI** (thinking): And at home, with everything ready, he can't stop thinking about her. If nothing matters, why does he feel sorry? Why does it hurt? The little girl has ruined his whole theory.

**ITSUKI** (thinking): There was a winter when I wanted nothing. Five years ago, after the prize, after Mum's diagnosis. I didn't want to die, exactly. I just didn't want. Not food, not books, not mornings. I lay on the floor of my flat and watched the ceiling get dark at four o'clock.

**ITSUKI** (thinking): What got me up was ridiculous. A woman from the flat below knocked to say my washing machine was leaking through her ceiling. She was so angry. She was the first person in a month who'd needed me to do something.

**ITSUKI** (thinking): That was the washing machine. That's how it broke. I never fixed it. I suppose I didn't want to throw away the thing that knocked on the door.

### [4:50] COIN LAUNDRY HOSHIZORA, INSIDE, NIGHT, SNOW

**MRS. HOSHINO**: Ah. Just you.

**ITSUKI**: Her mother's having the baby. Early.

**ITSUKI**: ...She rang you?

**MRS. HOSHINO**: She said, "Make sure he eats. And make sure he doesn't fix the washing machine." I said I'd do my best with the first one.

**MRS. HOSHINO**: Do you remember the first night you came in here? Three years ago. Three in the morning, in November. You had a carrier bag with one shirt and one sock in it, and a face like a wet newspaper.

**ITSUKI**: ...I didn't think anyone noticed.

**MRS. HOSHINO**: I was doing the coin boxes. You sat at that end of the bench for two hours watching one shirt go round. I thought, that boy's either in love or in trouble.

**ITSUKI**: Trouble.

**MRS. HOSHINO**: My husband and I opened this place in nineteen ninety-three. He used to say a coin laundry is the only honest room in Japan. Everybody's socks go round the same way. Company presidents and students and grandmothers, all watching the same window.

**MRS. HOSHINO**: He died in two thousand and nine. I kept it open twenty-four hours after that because I couldn't sleep anyway. Somebody might as well get some use out of me being awake.

**ITSUKI**: ...I'm glad you were awake.

**MRS. HOSHINO**: I know. You were back the next week with two shirts. That's how I knew you'd be all right. It was the extra shirt.

**MRS. HOSHINO**: She told me about you in September, you know. The girl. She said, "There's a man who reads White Nights wrong. Do you know if he's nice?" I said I'd known you three years and you'd never once said a whole sentence. She said, "Good. Then he's saving them."

**ITSUKI**: ...Oh.

**MRS. HOSHINO**: Eat something. There's a convenience store. I'll watch your sock.
