# Dostoevsky at the Coin Laundry E6: "The Prince of the Coin Laundry"

*November. A drunk salaryman who has missed the last train is asleep on the laundry bench, and Mio doesn't know what to do. Itsuki does. Watching him, she understands Dostoevsky's attempt to write a perfectly good man, and gives Itsuki the name that will stick. On the way out, he buys her a hot milk tea without asking.*

Book: The Idiot (1869). Format: anime, about 15 minutes. Original story. Discusses The Idiot (1869); Dostoevsky passages are our own renderings of the public-domain texts.

Original characters, designs and music. Not affiliated with any studio.

### [5:11] COIN LAUNDRY HOSHIZORA, INSIDE, NIGHT

**MIO**: He called you the prince.

**ITSUKI**: He was very drunk.

**MIO**: I've just finished the first half of The Idiot. Do you know what I thought, watching you? Prince Lev Nikolayevich Myshkin. Arrived in a coin laundry. Gives a stranger his coat.

**ITSUKI**: I'm not Myshkin.

**MIO**: You are absolutely Myshkin. That's your name now. Underground-san is retired. Prince.

**ITSUKI**: ...Please don't.

**MIO**: Prince. Prince. Prince of the Coin Laundry.

**ITSUKI** (thinking): It's going to stick. I can feel it sticking.

**MIO**: Okay. Seriously. Myshkin. He comes back to Petersburg from a clinic in Switzerland, where he's been treated for epilepsy since he was a boy. He has one bundle and no coat for the weather. Sound familiar.

**MIO**: And on the first day, everyone he meets thinks he's an idiot. Because he answers every question honestly. A general asks him something rude, and he just answers it. Like a child. Like there's nothing to protect.

**ITSUKI**: And then, by the end of the day, they're all telling him their secrets.

**MIO**: Yes! Why does that happen?

**ITSUKI**: Because he doesn't want anything from them. Everyone in Petersburg is trying to get money or a wife or a position. He's the only person in the room who isn't. People can feel that. It's like warmth from a stove.

**MIO**: Gramps's note is on the Swiss story. The girl, Marie.

**MIO**: A poor girl in the Swiss village everyone despised, because a man had used her and left her. Even her mother. And Myshkin gives her a kiss on the cheek and his last coin, and then, slowly, he teaches the village children to love her.

**ITSUKI**: She dies anyway.

**MIO**: She dies anyway. But not alone. The children carry her coffin.

**MIO**: And then there's Rogozhin. My favourite scary man. Black eyes, a fortune from his father, and he's obsessed with the same woman as Myshkin.

**ITSUKI**: And they swap crosses.

**MIO**: They swap crosses! Rogozhin's gold one for Myshkin's cheap tin one. They become brothers. And Rogozhin's old mother blesses the prince. And then, that same day, Rogozhin waits for him on a dark staircase with a knife.

**ITSUKI**: Myshkin sees the knife and says, "Parfyon, I don't believe it." And he has a seizure, and falls down the stairs, and that's what saves him.

**MIO**: Saved by being ill. By the thing that made everyone call him an idiot.

**ITSUKI**: And then the floor.

**MIO**: What's your answer?

**ITSUKI**: Not the beautiful woman. The book is clear about that. Her beauty doesn't save anyone, including her.
