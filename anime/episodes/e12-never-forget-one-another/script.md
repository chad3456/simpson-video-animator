# Dostoevsky at the Coin Laundry E12: "Let Us Never Forget One Another"

*Late March, the last night of the Coin Laundry Hoshizora, and the thirty-first Thursday. Mio leaves for Tokyo on Saturday. Over the last chapter of Dostoevsky's last novel, Alyosha's speech to twelve schoolboys at a stone, the two of them give each other the only presents that fit, and Itsuki finally fixes the sign.*

Book: The Brothers Karamazov (1880). Format: anime, about 15 minutes. Original story. Discusses The Brothers Karamazov (1880); Dostoevsky passages are our own renderings of the public-domain texts.

### [1:56] COIN LAUNDRY HOSHIZORA, INSIDE, NIGHT

*Thursday, late March. The last night.*

**MRS. HOSHINO**: Last night. Midnight, I lock the door, and the keys go to a man in a suit. Thirty-one years.

**MIO**: Are you sad?

**MRS. HOSHINO**: I'm seventy-six. I'm going to sleep for a week. Ask me in April.

**ITSUKI**: ...I'm always on time.

**MIO**: You were late in February. By four minutes.

**ITSUKI**: There was snow.

**MRS. HOSHINO**: Before you sit down. You've been suffering for three years. Go on.

**ITSUKI**: ...The sign?

**MRS. HOSHINO**: The sign. I've watched you look at it every week like it insulted your mother.

*INSERT: the dryer sign.*

**MIO**: Do it! Do it! Thirty-one weeks of pain!

*INSERT: the dryer sign.*

**ITSUKI**: ...Dryer, ten minutes. One hundred yen.

**MIO**: He's glowing! Mrs. Hoshino, look, he's actually glowing!

**MRS. HOSHINO**: Thirty-one years and nobody else ever noticed. My husband wrote that sign in nineteen ninety-three. He couldn't spell. I loved him anyway.

**MIO**: Did you see the notebook? On the tenth?

**ITSUKI**: "PASSED." In letters three lines high. With a drawing of a hedgehog wearing a graduation hat.

**MIO**: It's a very good hedgehog.

**ITSUKI**: Mrs. Hoshino cried. I saw her through the window. She pretended it was the steam from dryer three.

**MIO**: And you?

**ITSUKI**: ...It was very steamy that day.

**MIO**: Notice something? Only the baskets between us now. In August you sat so far away I thought I had a disease.

**ITSUKI**: ...The bench got shorter.

**MIO**: The bench did not get shorter!

**ITSUKI**: Mrs. Hoshino says it's the cold. Wood shrinks.

**MIO**: Okay. Last book. Last chapter. Last page. Gramps's favourite.

**ITSUKI**: The stone.

**MIO**: There's a boy called Ilyusha. Nine years old. His father is a poor ex-captain, and one day Dmitri drags his father out of a tavern by his beard, in front of the whole town, in front of the schoolboys. And little Ilyusha runs after them crying, and kisses Dmitri's hand, and begs him to let his father go.

**MIO**: And afterwards he sits on a big stone at the edge of town with his father, and says, Papa, when I grow up I'll challenge him and kill him. And he gets ill. Consumption. And all the boys in his class who used to throw stones at him come, one by one, and sit by his bed.

**ITSUKI**: Alyosha brings them. One at a time. He doesn't lecture anyone. He just keeps bringing them.

**MIO**: And Kolya brings the dog. Ilyusha thought he'd killed a dog, a long time ago, fed it bread with a pin in it. And it's been eating him up. And Kolya finds the dog, alive, and teaches it tricks, and brings it into the room and it does all its tricks for Ilyusha.

**ITSUKI**: Zhuchka.

**MIO**: Zhuchka! And Ilyusha's face. Gramps said he couldn't read that bit out loud without stopping.

**MIO**: And then Ilyusha dies. And after the funeral, Alyosha stops the boys by the stone. The same stone. And he gives them a speech. Twelve of them.

**ITSUKI**: He says, we'll be parting soon. And maybe we'll become bad people later. Cruel, or proud, or we'll laugh at people who suffer. But whatever happens, let's remember this. How we were all together, and how good it was, and how we loved that boy.

**ITSUKI**: He says one good memory, kept from when you were young, might be the thing that saves you one day. Even if it's only one. Even if you only remember it to laugh at it. Some part of you will say, no, I was good then. I was honest and good.

> Let us never forget one another. And whatever happens, let us remember how good it once was here, all of us together.

> *Grandfather's pencil:* This is the place / you can stand. / — G.

**MIO**: "This is the place you can stand." He wrote that when I was ten. After he read it to me.

**ITSUKI**: ...It's a good place.

**MIO**: And then Kolya asks Alyosha. Is it true, Karamazov, what religion says, that we'll all rise from the dead and see each other again? Even Ilyusha?

**ITSUKI**: And Alyosha says, certainly we shall. And we'll tell each other everything that happened, joyfully. Half laughing, half in ecstasy.

**MIO**: Half laughing. I like that he says half laughing. Like even in heaven people will be a bit embarrassed.

**ITSUKI**: ...Some people will be very embarrassed.

**MIO**: And then Kolya, the clever one, the show-off, shouts "Hurrah for Karamazov!" And all the boys shout it. And they go off to eat pancakes at the funeral lunch. That's the last line of Dostoevsky's last book. Pancakes.

**ITSUKI**: He finishes with lunch. After all of it. After the Inquisitor and the murder and the trial. Twelve boys going to eat pancakes.

**MIO**: It's the best ending in literature.

**ITSUKI**: It is.
