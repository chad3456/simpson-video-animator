# Dostoevsky at the Coin Laundry

*A twelve-episode slice-of-life anime in a soft, hand-painted look. Two lonely strangers, one shared author, and the Thursday nights in between.*

Original story, characters, designs and music. Dostoevsky's works are discussed and briefly quoted in our own renderings of the public-domain Russian texts. Not affiliated with any studio.

## Logline

Every Thursday night, in a 24-hour coin laundry by a canal, a shy 28-year-old proofreader who stopped writing and a teasing 17-year-old girl who inherited her grandfather's Dostoevsky books talk about one Dostoevsky character while their washing turns. Neither of them can say what the other has come to mean. Over one autumn and winter, the books say it for them.

## Where they meet

**Coin Laundry Hoshizora** (星空, "starry sky"), open 24 hours beside the Fujimi Bridge over the canal in Kawabe, a quiet provincial town. Six washers, four dryers (number 3 runs hot, so the stray cat sleeps on it), a long wooden bench, a vending machine outside with a bench under a streetlamp, and a cherry tree that marks the seasons. On the counter sits the **Laundry Notebook**, a lost-and-found book where customers leave notes about missing socks. It becomes the only channel the two of them have: they never exchange phone numbers.

The user's synopsis left the meeting place open ("one fine night they meet in the ..."). We chose the coin laundry because it gives two strangers a reason to sit side by side for forty minutes with nothing to do, every week, at night, in public; and because *White Nights*, the story of a lonely dreamer and a girl who meet at night and talk, is the first book they share.

## Tone

Inspired by the quiet, two-hander mood of shows like *Smoking Behind the Supermarket with You*: small talk that hides large feelings, long comfortable silences, running jokes, a teasing one and a flustered one, and a setting that becomes a sanctuary. Gentle humour, real sadness, no melodrama shortcuts.

**The feelings stay unspoken, and stay tender.** Mio has a crush she never names. Itsuki admires her, is grateful to her, and is keenly aware that she is a high-school student and he is a grown man; his restraint is part of his decency and part of why nothing is said. There is no romantic or physical consummation, ever. What grows between them is companionship, mutual rescue and a kind of love the books have words for and they do not. The ending is a parting, not a pairing.

## Characters

### Itsuki Kanzaki (神崎 樹), 28
- **Look:** tall, thin, a slight stoop; messy blue-black hair that falls into his eyes; round wire glasses; grey cardigan over a white shirt (autumn), a navy coat and an old wine-red scarf (winter). Carries a pencil behind his ear and a can of hot black coffee.
- **Life:** proofreader at Sakurai Printing, a small firm that prints textbooks and supermarket flyers. At 23 he won a literary magazine's newcomer prize for a novella, *The Room Without a Window*. He never finished a second book. Nobody at work knows. His washing machine broke three years ago and he never replaced it. His mother, Fumiko, lives in a care home in his hometown, two hours away by local train, with early-onset dementia; she sometimes calls him by his late father's name. He sends money and rarely visits, because it hurts.
- **Voice:** quiet, hesitant, trails off, apologizes too much, answers in one or two words. **His mechanic:** literature is the only door he can speak through. When a character in a book is at stake he becomes briefly, startlingly eloquent, then notices and goes red. Running gag: he hides his face behind whatever book he is holding.
- **What he wants:** to be left alone. **What he needs:** to risk something, to write again, to go and see his mother.
- **Arc:** from hiding (*White Nights*, *Underground*) to being seen (*The Idiot*), to writing again (*The Gambler*), to surviving being alone (*Ridiculous Man*), to telling someone he loves to leave (*Grand Inquisitor*), to going home to his mother (*Zosima*), to letting go well (*Alyosha*). He says Mio's first name exactly once, in the final episode.

### Mio Takanashi (小鳥遊 澪), 17
- **Look:** chestnut bob to the jaw with a side fringe and a small yellow star clip that was her grandfather's gift; bright, mischievous eyes; a mustard hoodie (autumn), a red duffle coat and white scarf (winter). Carries her grandfather's old hardbacks, bristling with paper slips.
- **Life:** third-year high-school student. Her grandfather, **Genzo Takanashi**, a minor translator of Russian literature, died in March. He raised her as much as anyone did; he taught her chess and to read with a pencil. She inherited his Dostoevsky shelf, full of his pencilled marginalia, pressed leaves and old receipts, and reads them to hear his voice. Her father left when she was small. This year her mother, **Kaori**, remarried a kind, awkward man, **Satoshi Ogawa**, and they moved into his house, where her grandfather's books sit in boxes in the corner of her small room. A baby is due in winter. She comes to the coin laundry on Thursdays because the house dryer is broken, and because Thursday is the night she can be out of the house without anyone asking why.
- **Voice:** quick, playful, teasing; nicknames everyone; laughs at her own pain before anyone else can. Reads her grandfather's margin notes aloud in a gruff voice.
- **Nicknames for Itsuki:** "Mr. Dreamer" (ep 1), "Underground-san" (ep 2), then "Prince" (ep 6 onward).
- **What she wants:** to be needed at home, so she can stop feeling like a guest. **What she needs:** permission to leave, to study Russian literature in Tokyo, in her grandfather's footsteps.
- **Arc:** jokes as armour (eps 1 to 3), the ugly thought she can't say (ep 4), being seen by someone else (eps 5 to 7), the deal that gets him writing (ep 8), the sister's birth (ep 9), choosing duty or freedom (ep 10), the exam and the discovery (ep 11), leaving (ep 12).

### Recurring
- **Fyodor** ("Fyodor Mikhailovich" to Mio, "Fedya" to Itsuki): a fat grey-and-white stray who sleeps on dryer 3. Never speaks. Judges everyone.
- **Mrs. Hoshino**, 70s, owner of the laundry. Comes some nights to empty the coin boxes. Dry, kind, misses nothing. In episode 5 she tells them the laundry closes at the end of March: the building is being sold. That is the series' clock.
- **Off-screen voices:** Mio's mother on the phone; Itsuki's mother at the care home (ep 11); Genzo, in memory (eps 1 and 12).

## Running devices
- **The gap on the bench.** They sit at opposite ends of the bench in episode 1. Each episode the gap is a little smaller. By episode 12 their laundry baskets are the only thing between them.
- **The Laundry Notebook.** Lost-sock notices become letters (ep 3, *Poor Folk*), a secret page (ep 9), and the last thing she leaves (ep 12).
- **The misspelled sign.** "DRYER 10 MINUTS / 100 YEN." The proofreader in him suffers every week. He fixes it in the final episode.
- **Grandfather's marginalia.** Each episode she reads one of Genzo's pencilled notes. They are the series' third voice, and the last one in the final episode is in Mio's own hand.
- **The count.** The Dreamer in *White Nights* got four nights. Mio keeps count of their Thursdays. They get thirty-one.
- **Coffee.** He always buys black coffee. In episode 6 he starts buying her a hot milk tea without asking. Neither mentions it.

## Episode shape (about 15 minutes)
1. **Cold open** (about 1 minute): an image and a line.
2. **Opening titles** (about 45 seconds): the canal, the laundry sign, the cat, the bench, over the main theme.
3. **Part A** (about 5 to 6 minutes), **eyecatch** (5 seconds), **Part B** (about 5 to 6 minutes).
4. **Ending titles** (about 40 seconds) over a still and the theme on solo piano, with the episode's Dostoevsky line.
5. **Next time** (about 20 seconds): Mio teases the next book.

## Look and sound
See `anime/STYLE.md`. Painted night skies and lamplight, cel-shaded characters with warm brown line art, slow drifting cameras, and an original piano-and-strings score (a waltz main theme, a sparse night theme, a playful theme for Mio's teasing, a minor theme for grief).
