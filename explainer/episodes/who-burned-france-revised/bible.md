# LUMEN Special 2, revised cut: "Who Burned France?" episode bible

**Event:** the October 2026 unrest in France. It grew out of the school strikes that began in September 2026 and peaked with the national protest day on Saturday 17 October. All figures are as of 23 October 2026, while inquiries are still open.

**Brief:** the requester asked for an episode on how, "under the garb of student protest", immigrants burned France, using maps and reports, especially Asra Nomani's coverage. The episode treats that sentence as a claim to test. It attributes the claim to the people who made it, then sets it against what officials, prosecutors, reporters and fact-checkers have published.

**Format:** a single in-depth explainer, about 14 minutes, rated adult because it shows violence, arson and injured children. All imagery is stylised: no gore, no real people drawn, no logos. It uses the same narrator, score and creatures as the other LUMEN episodes.

## Thesis and hook

- **Thesis:** France did burn, and the people who burned it were real. They were violent fringes, opportunists and looters who used a genuine student movement as cover, and adult political networks amplified it. Published evidence does not show that "immigrants" as a group did it. The second fire, online, was built largely from fakes.
- **Hook:** the 1883 Limoges town hall on fire, then a feed of five competing stories about who lit it.

## The claim test (Part 3 and Part 6)

| Part of the claim | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Violent people used the student protests as cover | **Supported** | 70% adults in weekend custody; Limoges suspect a 40-year-old activist; masked groups in Paris; officials' own words |
| Adult political networks pushed the movement | **Supported, with a caveat** | Nomani's group counts; Saint-Denis mayor; but organising is not the same as burning, and LFI disputes it |
| Immigrants as a group burned France | **Not supported** | No prosecutor or official has said so; AP found no evidence; ethnicity is not recorded; the one identified arsonist is a French far-left activist |
| Who exactly was arrested | **Still unknown** | Full profiles not published; inquiries open |

Nomani's reporting is shown fairly: she documents organising networks. She does not claim that immigrants did the burning, and the episode says so.

## The lens (closing scene)

Saad's "suicidal empathy" is used in both directions. One trap is to excuse violence because it wears a student's coat. The mirror-image trap is to blame millions of neighbours for crimes they did not commit. Both are labelled as our reading of his lens, not his words.

## Fairness rules applied

- Rioters are hooded grey pebbles: anonymous, never marked by religion, skin or nationality. Police wear caps. Pupils, staff and residents are the usual bright pebbles.
- Every count is on screen with a source chip. Where sources disagree (550,000 vs 800,000; "known to police" vs "first offenders"), both are shown.
- Claims are quoted and attributed. Each is then tested against the record.
- Fakes are shown with their view counts and a "debunked" stamp. Their imagery is redrawn in LUMEN's own style and is never reproduced.
