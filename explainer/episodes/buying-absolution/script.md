## [0:52] Part 1: Opportunity or Outcome?


_[0:56] Scene `race`: land / day; on screen: label, pebble, shape._

- `0:56` Saad's argument starts with a distinction. Equality of opportunity means everyone runs the race under the same rules.
- `1:04` Equality of outcome means everyone should finish together. In 2020, Kamala Harris shared a video describing equity as treatment where everyone ends up in the same place.
- `1:17` Saad argues that because people differ in talents, interests and choices, equal outcomes can only be produced by treating people unequally.
- `1:26` Supporters reply that people don't all start from the same line either, and a little help at the start is simply fairness.

## [1:34] Part 2: DIE in Hiring


_[1:38] Scene `statements`: studio / midnight; on screen: bars, label._

- `1:38` Much of his evidence comes from universities. One analysis he cites found that about one in five American faculty job ads asked applicants for a diversity statement.
- `1:50` In a Canadian sample, nearly all of them did: four hundred and seventy-seven out of four hundred and eighty-nine.

_[1:58] Scene `chairs`: city / plum; on screen: card, label._

- `1:58` Some Canadian research chairs were restricted to candidates from particular groups, and the University of Michigan was reported to employ hundreds of DEI staff, at a cost of tens of millions of dollars a year.
- `2:12` Saad calls these indulgences: institutions pay to feel absolved, and students and taxpayers pick up the bill.

_[2:20] Scene `tests`: studio / teal; on screen: card, icon, label._

- `2:20` Many universities also dropped standardized tests, in the name of fairness.
- `2:26` Interestingly, several, including MIT, Dartmouth and Yale, later brought them back, saying test scores actually helped them find talented students from poor backgrounds.

## [2:37] Part 3: When Lives Are at Stake


_[2:41] Scene `medicine`: studio / plum; on screen: card, icon, label._

- `2:41` Saad is most alarmed when this reaches medicine and safety.
- `2:45` He points to a proposed update of Canada's framework for training doctors, which he says puts values like anti-oppression ahead of medical expertise, and to a 2022 University of Minnesota medical school oath that pledged to honour Indigenous ways of healing and to fight white supremacy.

_[3:06] Scene `faa`: city / midnight; on screen: icon, label, quote, text._

- `3:06` He cites air traffic controller hiring at the US Federal Aviation Administration, which in 2014 introduced a questionnaire that screened out many top graduates of aviation programs. The agency was later sued over it.
- `3:22` And during the Los Angeles fires of January 2025, earlier remarks by a deputy fire chief went viral. Asked whether she could carry a man out of a fire, she had said he got himself in the wrong place if she had to carry him out.
- `3:38` Saad's point is simple: when lives are at stake, skill has to come first.

## [3:43] Part 4: Go Woke, Go Broke?


_[3:47] Scene `brands`: city / ember; on screen: card, label._

- `3:47` Then there's woke capitalism. In 2023, Bud Light sent a personalised can to a transgender influencer. A boycott followed, and sales fell so far that it lost its place as America's best-selling beer.
- `4:03` In 2024, Jaguar unveiled a rebrand with a bright, stylish ad that showed no cars at all. It was widely mocked.
- `4:12` Saad's verdict: when companies sell virtue instead of products, customers notice.

## [4:18] Part 5: The Case Against


_[4:22] Scene `against`: studio / midnight; on screen: label, scale._

- `4:22` Critics have answers.
- `4:24` Discrimination hasn't vanished. Studies that send out identical CVs under different names still find gaps in who gets called back.
- `4:33` Outreach that widens the pool of applicants doesn't lower the bar. And the research on diverse teams, while mixed, isn't nothing.
- `4:42` Many companies are simply responding to customers. And some DEI programs really were badly designed, which plenty of their own supporters admit.
- `4:52` Shared ground: open the doors as wide as you can, then pick the best person who walks through them.
