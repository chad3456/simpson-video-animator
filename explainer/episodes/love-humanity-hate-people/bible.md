# LUMEN explainer: "Love Humanity, Hate People"

*Dostoevsky's field guide to rebels, saviours and moral gatekeepers.* About 11 minutes, paper-collage look plus a new diagram kit (`explainer/engine/vox.js`): ink-blue grounds with dashed registration grids, paper tables that fill row by row, philosophy maps whose arrows draw themselves, trait bars and hand-set equations. The look takes its cues from the reference reels (collage portraits, bold condensed type, blue dashed-grid frames, highlighter on quotes) without copying any frame.

## Thesis (one sentence)

Dostoevsky, a former member of a secret revolutionary group himself, shows again and again that people who love "humanity in general" can come to despise people in particular, and that a cause which gives you permission to stop seeing the person in front of you will turn a moralist into a nihilist.

## The hook

Why do people who set out to free humanity end up despising humans, and why do the loudest guardians of morality so often believe in nothing?

## How it differs from the earlier "Demons" episode

The Demons documentary told one novel's story (Nechayev, the cell of five, Stavrogin, Kirillov). This episode is a cross-book taxonomy: five types of rebel from five works, compared in tables and maps, with modern mirrors and a full case against Dostoevsky. Demons appears only as "type three" (the shame mechanism and Shigalyov's formula), with a pointer to the earlier episode.

## The case against (given its own scene)

1. Caricature: many radicals were brave, self-sacrificing and right about serfdom and censorship.
2. His own politics: a monarchist and nationalist, with antisemitic passages in *A Writer's Diary*; his remedy was a political program too.
3. Rebellion is not the disease (Camus).
4. He argued against himself: the rebels get the strongest lines (Bakhtin's polyphony; Ivan is never refuted in debate).

**Shared ground:** the problem isn't wanting justice. It's the moment a cause gives you permission to stop seeing the person in front of you.

## Fairness notes

- Modern mirrors are named in neutral, cross-partisan terms (purge, struggle session, online pile-on; "offered by every side"), never acted out by stand-ins for real groups.
