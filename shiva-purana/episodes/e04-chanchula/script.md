# Shiva Purana · Issue Four: Chanchula

*A wife, a ghost, and the power of a story*

A woman who wasted her life hears a few words of Shiva's story and turns around. Then she goes back for the husband who became a ghost.

Source: Shiva Purana, Shiva Purana Mahatmya, chapters 3 to 5 (tr. Bibek Debroy)

### Scene: Naimisha forest `0:08`

**Sage:** Suta, you say that simply hearing Shiva's story can free a person. Even a sinner?

**Suta:** Even a sinner. Listen to the story of Chanchula, and her husband Binduga.

## Issue Four: Chanchula `0:20`

## Part One: The Village of Bashkala `0:27`

### Scene: Bashkala, by the sea `0:32`

*Narrator:* By the sea lay a village called Bashkala, where people cheated and quarrelled and cared for nothing but pleasure.

*Narrator:* There lived a brahmin named Binduga. He left his faithful wife Chanchula and spent his days with a courtesan.

**Chanchula:** You abandon me for another woman, day after day. What am I to do, alone in this house?

**Binduga:** Do as you please. Find your own company. Just bring the money to me.

*Narrator:* And so, for years, the two of them lived without shame, without dharma, chasing pleasure and money.

### Scene: death `1:05`

*Narrator:* Binduga died. After long torments in hell, he was reborn as a terrible pishacha, a ghost, wandering the Vindhya mountains.

## Part Two: A Few Words at Gokarna `1:16`

### Scene: Gokarna, years later `1:21`

*Narrator:* Years later, her youth gone, Chanchula went with her relatives to the holy place of Gokarna. In a temple, a brahmin was reciting the Shiva Purana.

**The Reciter:** Those who throw away their dharma for pleasure meet Yama's messengers at the end. And what waits for them there is terrible.

*Narrator:* Chanchula began to tremble.

**Chanchula:** Brahmin, save me! I have wasted my whole youth. When Yama's messengers come for me, who will save me?

**Chanchula:** You are my guru, my mother, my father. I seek refuge with you.

**The Reciter:** Do not be afraid. Your understanding has woken at the right time, by Shiva's grace.

**The Reciter:** Repentance is the greatest atonement. And Shiva's story cleans the mind, the way a cloth cleans a mirror.

### Scene: listen `2:10`

*Narrator:* So she stayed, and served him, and listened. Day after day, the whole Shiva Purana.

*Narrator:* Her mind grew still. She meditated on Shiva's form, with the moon in his hair and the trident in his hand.

### Scene: ascent `2:25`

*Narrator:* When her time came, there were no messengers of Yama. Instead, a shining chariot came down from Shiva's world.

*Narrator:* Her body became radiant. And she rose with Shiva's attendants to the world of Shiva and Parvati.

## Part Three: The Ghost of the Vindhyas `2:43`

### Scene: Shiva's world `2:48`

*Narrator:* Chanchula served the goddess Parvati with love. But one thing troubled her.

**Chanchula:** Mother, where is my husband Binduga now? What has become of him?

**Parvati:** He suffered long in hell. Now he is a pishacha in the Vindhya mountains, living on wind, in torment.

**Chanchula:** However wicked he was, he was my husband. Mother, is there no way to save him?

**Parvati:** There is. The same way you were saved. He must hear Shiva's story.

**Parvati:** Tumburu, sweetest of singers. Go with her to the Vindhyas, and sing him the Shiva Purana.

### Scene: The Vindhya mountains `3:22`

**Binduga:** Who dares come here? Go away!

**Tumburu:** Sit, Binduga. I have not come to fight. I have come to sing.

*Narrator:* Tumburu played his veena and told the story of Shiva. Of the pillar of fire, of the thief and the lamp, of Shiva's mercy without end.

*Narrator:* And slowly, the ghost stopped growling, and knelt, and listened.

**Binduga:** Chanchula. You came back for me. After everything.

**Chanchula:** It was Shiva's story that saved us both. Come.

*Narrator:* Free of his ghostly body, Binduga rose with them in a heavenly chariot, to the feet of Shiva.

### Scene: close `3:59`

**Suta:** That is why we tell these stories. A few words reached Chanchula at the right time, and changed everything.

**Sage:** Now tell us of Shiva himself, Suta. Of his first wife, Sati.

**Suta:** The daughter of Daksha. Yes. That is a story of love, and of fire.
